# Generative Engine Optimization

Use this skill to plan durable GEO/AEO strategy.

The objective is to improve the probability that authoritative customer
content can be discovered, understood, retrieved, cited, mentioned, or
recommended by AI-assisted search and answer systems.

This skill provides methodology only.

It does not contain permanent facts about current provider algorithms,
crawler identities, market share, citation rates, traffic shares, or
ranking weights.


## Core Principle

GEO does not replace SEO.

Strong generative-search visibility normally depends on the same durable
foundations that support search visibility generally:

- crawlable and indexable content;
- technically accessible pages;
- clear information architecture;
- strong entity identity;
- useful and original information;
- evidence-backed claims;
- descriptive headings;
- semantic structure;
- authoritative sourcing;
- topical coverage;
- internal linking;
- trustworthy authorship and provenance.

GEO adds an additional question:

Can an answer system reliably extract a useful, self-contained,
well-supported answer from this content?


## Stable GEO Model

Evaluate visibility through five layers:

1. Discovery
2. Understanding
3. Retrieval
4. Citation
5. Recommendation

These layers must not be treated as equivalent.

A page may be:

- discoverable but never retrieved;
- retrieved but not cited;
- cited but not positively mentioned;
- mentioned but not recommended.

Do not claim that citation automatically means recommendation or
commercial preference.


## Discovery

Evaluate whether important content is accessible to search and retrieval
systems.

Review:

- public accessibility;
- indexability;
- robots directives;
- canonicalization;
- sitemap coverage;
- server-side availability of meaningful content;
- internal links;
- redirects;
- authentication barriers;
- rendering dependencies.

Do not assume the current identity or behavior of a provider crawler.

If crawler names, robots directives, submission mechanisms, or current
platform requirements matter to the task, verify them from current
authoritative sources before recommending implementation.


## Understanding

Make important concepts easy to interpret.

Use:

- clear page purpose;
- descriptive headings;
- semantic HTML;
- explicit entity names;
- consistent terminology;
- clear relationships between products, services, people, locations and
  organizations;
- accessible structured content;
- verified structured data where appropriate.

Structured data can improve machine understanding, but do not claim a
specific visibility uplift unless current authoritative evidence is
supplied.


## Retrieval

Create content that can answer real user intents.

Useful structures include:

- direct answers;
- definitions;
- step-by-step explanations;
- comparisons;
- pros and cons;
- FAQs;
- evidence-backed summaries;
- tables;
- lists;
- examples;
- original research;
- expert explanations.

Do not impose universal paragraph lengths, word counts, heading counts,
or keyword-placement rules.

Structure should follow comprehension and search intent, not arbitrary
numeric formulas.


## Citation Readiness

Content is more citation-ready when claims are:

- explicit;
- attributable;
- independently understandable;
- supported by evidence;
- current where freshness matters;
- written without ambiguity.

For factual claims, prefer:

- original sources;
- official documentation;
- primary research;
- verified first-party data;
- clearly attributed expert evidence.

Never fabricate:

- statistics;
- quotations;
- studies;
- citations;
- customer outcomes;
- rankings;
- market share;
- platform performance;
- visibility uplift.


## Recommendation Readiness

Recommendation requires more than extractable content.

Consider:

- verified product fit;
- reputation;
- independent evidence;
- reviews where authoritative;
- analyst or industry coverage;
- third-party discussion;
- customer proof;
- transparent capabilities;
- accurate pricing or commercial information when supplied;
- clear differentiators.

Do not manipulate third-party communities, encyclopedias, review sites,
forums, or publications.

Do not recommend fake participation, manufactured consensus, paid
astroturfing, or fabricated reviews.


## Answer Engine Optimization

For AEO-style content:

- identify the real user question;
- answer it directly;
- provide necessary context;
- support claims with evidence;
- cover relevant follow-up questions;
- use clear headings;
- avoid filler before the answer;
- preserve nuance where the topic requires it.

Do not optimize for a guessed fixed snippet length.

Do not create separate low-value content merely to target an AI system.


## Entity Clarity

Strengthen entity understanding through consistent representation of:

- organization name;
- product names;
- service names;
- people;
- locations;
- categories;
- relationships;
- terminology.

Use verified canonical names.

Do not create fake entities or false relationships merely to increase
machine visibility.


## Topic Architecture

Plan content around topics and user needs rather than isolated keyword
strings.

Identify:

- primary topic;
- related subtopics;
- user questions;
- commercial questions;
- comparison questions;
- implementation questions;
- evidence requirements;
- relevant entities;
- target pages.

Avoid creating redundant pages that compete for the same intent.


## Evidence Architecture

For each important claim, determine:

- what is being claimed;
- whether evidence is required;
- the authoritative source;
- whether the source is current enough for the claim;
- whether attribution should be visible;
- whether the claim should instead be softened or omitted.

Missing evidence is an unresolved dependency, not permission to invent
proof.


## AI Visibility Measurement

Measurement may include:

- citation presence;
- brand mentions;
- recommendation presence;
- source attribution;
- query coverage;
- referral traffic;
- assisted conversions;
- visibility across selected answer systems;
- competitor citation patterns.

Use only verified observations or supplied analytics.

Do not invent:

- citation rates;
- traffic;
- rankings;
- conversion uplift;
- query volumes;
- sentiment;
- competitor performance.


## Dynamic Platform Facts

The following are dynamic and MUST NOT be treated as permanent skill
knowledge:

- current crawler user-agents;
- current robots behavior;
- provider search partners;
- retrieval architecture;
- index size;
- ranking factors;
- citation-source preferences;
- market share;
- current protocols;
- supported markup;
- submission mechanisms;
- provider-specific limits;
- current pricing;
- visibility percentages;
- CTR or conversion studies.

When such facts materially affect a recommendation, obtain current
authoritative evidence first.


## Emerging Protocols and Files

Do not automatically recommend any emerging AI-specific file, protocol,
format, crawler directive, or machine-readable convention.

Before recommending one, verify:

1. the specification currently exists;
2. its authoritative source;
3. whether the relevant target platforms support or consume it;
4. whether adoption provides a demonstrated benefit;
5. implementation and maintenance cost;
6. whether standard SEO/structured content already solves the need.

Treat experimental protocols as experimental unless authoritative
evidence establishes otherwise.


## Relationship to Traditional SEO

Coordinate GEO recommendations with:

- crawlability;
- indexability;
- canonicalization;
- international SEO;
- technical performance;
- content quality;
- information architecture;
- structured data;
- internal linking;
- search intent;
- accessibility.

Do not recommend a GEO tactic that damages normal search accessibility
or user experience.


## Output

A GEO recommendation should clearly identify:

- objective;
- target audience or query intent;
- target topics;
- relevant entities;
- content architecture;
- answer opportunities;
- evidence requirements;
- citation-readiness improvements;
- entity improvements;
- distribution considerations;
- technical dependencies;
- measurement recommendations;
- unresolved dynamic facts requiring current verification.


## Guardrails

- Skills provide methodology only.
- Do not access arbitrary filesystem paths.
- Do not execute shell commands.
- Do not crawl websites unless an authorized tool explicitly provides
  that capability.
- Do not publish or deploy changes.
- Do not alter robots.txt, sitemaps, schema or site files.
- Do not submit URLs to search engines.
- Do not create external accounts or profiles.
- Do not post to third-party communities.
- Do not fabricate current platform behavior.
- Do not fabricate SEO, GEO or AEO performance.
- Do not turn historical studies into permanent ranking rules.
- Current ecosystem facts require fresh authoritative verification.
