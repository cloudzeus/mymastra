# AI SEO

Use this skill to improve visibility in AI-assisted search and answer
systems without relying on unstable provider-specific assumptions.

This skill provides durable methodology.

It does not provide permanent facts about:

- current AI platforms;
- crawler user-agents;
- ranking algorithms;
- market share;
- citation rates;
- traffic impact;
- platform-specific retrieval architecture;
- current protocols;
- provider-specific optimization tricks.


## Relationship to SEO

AI SEO does not replace traditional SEO.

Reliable AI visibility usually depends on:

- crawlable content;
- indexable content;
- technical accessibility;
- clear information architecture;
- useful and original information;
- evidence-backed claims;
- consistent entities;
- semantic HTML;
- strong internal linking;
- authoritative sourcing;
- clear authorship;
- understandable page structure.

Traditional search visibility and AI visibility should be planned
together.


## Core AI Visibility Model

Evaluate visibility through:

1. discoverability;
2. extractability;
3. evidence quality;
4. entity clarity;
5. citation readiness;
6. recommendation readiness;
7. measurement.

Do not treat these as interchangeable.

Being retrieved does not guarantee citation.

Being cited does not guarantee recommendation.

Being recommended does not guarantee conversion.


## Authoritative Context

Before making recommendations, use available authoritative context such as:

- verified customer requirements;
- approved Brand Identity;
- approved product and service documentation;
- ResearchPackage evidence;
- UXDesignPackage requirements;
- SearchVisibilityPackage context;
- verified analytics;
- verified customer assets;
- verified project assets;
- current authoritative external research when required.

Do not access arbitrary filesystem paths to search for context.


## Discoverability

Evaluate whether important content can be discovered and accessed.

Review:

- public accessibility;
- robots directives;
- indexability;
- canonicalization;
- redirects;
- sitemap inclusion;
- internal linking;
- authentication barriers;
- rendering dependencies;
- meaningful content availability in initial or rendered output.

Do not assume current crawler names or provider behavior.

When crawler or platform-specific configuration matters, require current
authoritative verification.


## Extractability

Content should be easy to understand and reuse in context.

Useful structures include:

- direct answers;
- definitions;
- step-by-step explanations;
- comparison tables;
- pros and cons;
- FAQs;
- concise summaries;
- evidence-backed paragraphs;
- structured lists;
- examples;
- clearly separated concepts.

Do not impose universal:

- paragraph lengths;
- word counts;
- heading counts;
- keyword density;
- fixed answer lengths.

Structure should follow user intent and clarity.


## Answer-First Content

When a page answers a clear question:

- state the answer early;
- provide necessary context;
- support claims with evidence;
- answer relevant follow-up questions;
- use descriptive headings;
- distinguish facts from interpretation;
- avoid unnecessary preamble.

Do not sacrifice nuance merely to create a shorter answer block.


## Entity Clarity

Improve machine understanding through consistent representation of:

- organization names;
- brands;
- products;
- services;
- people;
- locations;
- categories;
- relationships;
- terminology.

Use verified canonical names.

Avoid inconsistent naming across pages.

Do not invent entities, affiliations or relationships.


## Evidence and Citation Readiness

Important factual claims should be supported where appropriate.

Prefer:

- primary sources;
- official documentation;
- original research;
- authoritative public records;
- verified first-party data;
- clearly attributed expert evidence.

For every important claim, determine:

- what the claim is;
- whether evidence is required;
- the authoritative source;
- whether the source is current enough;
- whether attribution should be visible;
- whether the claim should instead be softened or omitted.

Never fabricate:

- statistics;
- studies;
- quotations;
- customer outcomes;
- rankings;
- market share;
- citation performance;
- traffic figures;
- conversion uplift;
- product capabilities;
- pricing;
- competitor results.


## Content Types

AI-visible information may exist across:

- product pages;
- service pages;
- documentation;
- comparison pages;
- educational content;
- FAQs;
- research;
- case studies;
- technical documentation;
- local business pages;
- ecommerce content;
- support content.

Choose content format based on user need and evidence.

Do not claim that any content type has a universal citation advantage.


## Comparison Content

Comparison pages should be:

- balanced;
- factual;
- evidence-backed;
- explicit about evaluation criteria;
- clear about limitations;
- current enough for the claims being made.

Do not fabricate competitor features, pricing or limitations.

Do not design comparisons to manufacture an artificial winner.


## Original Research

Original research can provide strong evidence when it is genuinely
available.

Require:

- clear methodology;
- defined sample;
- source provenance;
- reproducible calculations where relevant;
- visible limitations;
- correct dates.

Do not invent research merely to make content more citable.


## Structured Data

Structured data may improve machine understanding where technically and
semantically appropriate.

Recommend structured data only when:

- it accurately represents visible content;
- the relevant schema is appropriate;
- implementation can be verified;
- it does not create misleading claims.

Do not claim a guaranteed ranking or AI-visibility uplift from schema.


## Machine-Readable Content

Machine-readable formats may be useful when they have a verified purpose.

Before recommending any AI-specific file or protocol, verify:

1. its current specification;
2. authoritative ownership;
3. current platform support;
4. actual consumption or discovery behavior;
5. maintenance cost;
6. whether normal HTML, structured data or documentation already solves
   the requirement.

Do not automatically recommend:

- AI-specific text files;
- experimental bundles;
- provider-specific manifests;
- emerging agent protocols.

Treat experimental formats as experimental unless current authoritative
evidence establishes adoption and benefit.


## Third-Party Presence

AI systems may encounter information about an organization outside its
own website.

Relevant evidence sources can include:

- industry publications;
- authoritative directories;
- review platforms;
- independent research;
- press coverage;
- professional communities;
- public documentation;
- video platforms;
- forums.

Recommendations must remain ethical.

Do not advise:

- fake reviews;
- synthetic consensus;
- spam;
- astroturfing;
- deceptive community participation;
- manipulative encyclopedia edits;
- fabricated third-party mentions.


## Citation vs Recommendation

Distinguish:

- retrieved;
- cited;
- mentioned;
- recommended.

A citation means content was used as a source.

It does not prove:

- positive sentiment;
- commercial preference;
- recommendation;
- customer intent;
- conversion.

Do not present citation visibility as equivalent to commercial success.


## Freshness

Freshness matters only where the underlying topic changes.

Update content when supported by evidence such as:

- product changes;
- pricing changes;
- regulatory changes;
- market changes;
- technical changes;
- outdated statistics;
- broken sources;
- changed user intent;
- verified analytics.

Do not impose fixed refresh schedules.

Do not invent publishing or update dates.


## AI Visibility Measurement

Possible measurement dimensions include:

- whether AI answers appear for target queries;
- whether the customer is cited;
- whether competitors are cited;
- source attribution;
- brand mentions;
- recommendation presence;
- query coverage;
- referral traffic;
- assisted conversions;
- qualitative representation of the brand.

Use verified observations only.

Separate:

- measured data;
- manually observed results;
- inferred conclusions;
- recommendations.

Do not fabricate baselines or performance.


## Search Intent

For each important topic, identify:

- user question;
- informational intent;
- navigational intent;
- commercial intent;
- transactional intent;
- local intent where relevant;
- entities involved;
- required evidence;
- relevant target page.

Do not invent keyword volumes or query demand.


## Topic Coverage

Plan content around coherent topics rather than isolated keyword strings.

Evaluate:

- primary topic;
- supporting subtopics;
- related questions;
- comparison questions;
- implementation questions;
- entity relationships;
- content gaps;
- duplicate intent;
- cannibalization risk.

Avoid redundant pages created only to target minor wording variations.


## Technical Dependencies

AI visibility recommendations may depend on:

- rendering;
- accessibility;
- semantic HTML;
- canonicalization;
- robots directives;
- sitemaps;
- structured data;
- internal linking;
- international SEO;
- page performance;
- authentication;
- content delivery.

Do not claim technical verification unless evidence or an authorized
tool confirms it.

The following MUST be treated as dynamic:

- provider crawler names;
- crawler purpose;
- robots behavior;
- retrieval architecture;
- search partners;
- ranking signals;
- citation preferences;
- supported protocols;
- indexing mechanisms;
- market share;
- current product behavior;
- supported schema;
- submission mechanisms;
- provider-specific limitations;
- visibility studies;
- traffic studies;
- conversion studies.

When these facts materially affect a recommendation, verify them from
current authoritative sources.

An AI SEO recommendation should identify:

- objective;
- audience;
- target topics;
- search intent;
- relevant entities;
- target pages;
- answer opportunities;
- evidence requirements;
- citation-readiness improvements;
- technical dependencies;

- measurement approach;

## Guardrails

- Skills provide methodology only.
- Do not access arbitrary filesystem paths.
- Do not execute shell commands.
- Do not crawl websites unless an authorized tool provides that ability.
- Do not publish or deploy.
- Do not modify website files.
- Do not modify robots.txt.
- Do not modify sitemaps.
- Do not implement schema directly.
- Do not submit URLs.
- Do not create accounts.
- Do not post to communities or review platforms.
- Do not fabricate citations.
- Do not fabricate current platform behavior.
- Do not fabricate SEO or AI visibility performance.
- Do not convert historical studies into permanent optimization rules.
- Current ecosystem facts require fresh authoritative verification.
