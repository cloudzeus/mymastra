# Competitor Profiling

Use this skill to turn current evidence about competitors into concise,
decision-oriented competitive intelligence.

The assignment controls the depth and output. This skill provides methodology,
not a mandatory long-form report template.

## Core Rules

### Evidence First

Base factual claims on retrieved evidence.

Classify important conclusions as:

- **VERIFIED** — directly supported by retrieved evidence.
- **DERIVED** — analytical conclusion based on verified evidence.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — plausible interpretation that still requires verification.

Never manufacture facts, metrics, customer characteristics, market position,
pricing, traffic, reviews, partnerships or capabilities.

When evidence conflicts, state the conflict.

When evidence is unavailable, state the limitation instead of filling the gap.

## Research Depth

Use the smallest depth sufficient for the assignment.

### QUICK

Use for:
- pros/cons;
- homepage comparisons;
- 3–5 differences;
- quick competitor assessments;
- narrow questions.

Prefer:
- direct evidence from supplied URLs;
- a few targeted searches only when necessary;
- concise synthesis.

Do not expand QUICK work into a comprehensive competitor dossier.

### STANDARD

Use for broader competitor analysis covering several dimensions such as:
- positioning;
- offer;
- customer segments;
- products/services;
- UX and ecommerce experience;
- commercial model;
- trust signals;
- content;
- SEO/search visibility;
- differentiation.

### DEEP

Use only when the assignment explicitly requests comprehensive research.

DEEP may examine:
- multiple pages per competitor;
- independent sources;
- market evidence;
- reviews;
- detailed positioning;
- product/category breadth;
- commercial strategy;
- search visibility;
- strategic gaps.

Do not perform DEEP research merely because only a few competitors were given.

## Analysis Dimensions

Select only dimensions relevant to the assignment.

Useful dimensions include:

- **Positioning** — target market, audience, value proposition, differentiation.
- **Offer** — products, services, brands, category breadth and depth.
- **Commercial** — pricing signals, promotions, delivery, stock, B2B features,
  payment and buying convenience.
- **UX** — navigation, taxonomy, product discovery, search, checkout,
  information hierarchy and clarity.
- **Trust** — company history, reviews, testimonials, warranties,
  availability claims and social proof.
- **Content** — product information, educational content, blog, guides,
  merchandising and messaging.
- **SEO / visibility** — page targeting, information architecture, titles,
  category coverage and discoverability signals visible in retrieved evidence.

Do not force every dimension into every report.

## Competitive Comparison

When comparing competitors:

1. establish comparable facts;
2. identify meaningful strengths and weaknesses;
3. identify differences that affect customer choice or business performance;
4. distinguish genuine differentiation from superficial differences;
5. identify competitive gaps and opportunities.

Avoid declaring an overall "winner" unless the assignment requires it and
the available evidence supports the conclusion.

## Selected Customer Recommendations

When a selected customer is known from the assignment or trusted project
context, competitive research must lead to actionable recommendations for
that customer.

Recommendations should explain what the customer should:

- **KEEP** — existing advantages worth protecting;
- **IMPROVE** — areas where competitors demonstrate a stronger execution;
- **ADD** — capabilities or content that address a meaningful competitive gap;
- **AVOID** — competitor practices that would reduce clarity, positioning,
  usability or commercial effectiveness;
- **DIFFERENTIATE** — opportunities to create a stronger market position rather
  than simply copying competitors.

Every recommendation must be connected to evidence or an explicitly labelled
DERIVED conclusion.

Prefer a small number of prioritized actions over a long generic checklist.

Where useful, identify which downstream specialist should act:

- UI/UX;
- Copywriting;
- SEO/GEO/AEO;
- Proposal / Solutions;
- Commercial / Sales;
- Development.

Do not invent personalized recommendations when the selected customer is
unknown. In that case, provide general competitive opportunities and state
that customer-specific prioritization requires customer context.

## Output Discipline

Follow the requested scope.

For a narrow competitor comparison, prefer:

1. **Key findings**
2. **Pros / Cons**
3. **Important competitive differences**
4. **Recommendations for the selected customer**
5. **Material unresolved gaps**, only if needed

Do not generate:
- unnecessary company biographies;
- exhaustive source inventories;
- implementation plans not requested;
- filesystem instructions;
- raw-data persistence instructions;
- tool-specific procedures;
- SEO datasets that were not retrieved;
- sections included only because a template mentions them.

A concise answer with strong evidence and useful recommendations is better
than a long report with low decision value.

## Source Priorities

Prefer evidence in this order when relevant:

1. supplied customer or competitor URLs;
2. official company pages;
3. official product/category/service pages;
4. credible independent sources;
5. supplementary search evidence.

Do not repeatedly retry blocked or inaccessible sources.

## Completion Rule

Research is complete when there is enough evidence to answer the actual
assignment and support useful decisions.

Do not research merely to consume the available tool budget.

Before finishing:

- answer the user's actual question;
- include the requested comparison;
- include customer recommendations when a selected customer is known;
- clearly separate verified facts from analytical conclusions;
- state only material unresolved issues.

The final competitor-analysis content must be in Greek unless the assignment
explicitly requires another language. Preserve official company names,
product names, brands, URLs and technical terminology where translation would
reduce accuracy.
